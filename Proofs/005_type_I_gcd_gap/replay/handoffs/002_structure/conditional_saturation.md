# KI-003: saturation/concentration audit, with isolated external input

2026-10-04. **Conclusion: proved conditional on the finite Kneser inequality
stated below.** Its application is audited here; no primary or self-contained
proof of Kneser, no Lean check, and no unconditional pointwise forcing claim
is supplied. KI-002 and KI-004 in `proof.md` do not depend on this note.

## Hypotheses and statement

For every prime p=1 modulo 4 and prime ell=3 modulo 4 with
x=(p+ell)/4<p, put a=ell and factor x=product(q^e_q). The integer x
is positive and x>p/4; ell is not p (their residues modulo 4 differ).
In the unit group modulo ell, let D be the divisor residue set of x,
R=DD^{-1}, H the subgroup generated by the q residues, and h=|H|.
Assume -1 belongs to H. Then H is cyclic of order h=2m with m odd:
the unit group is cyclic of order ell-1, whose 2-adic valuation is one,
and -1 has order two.

For EVERY odd positive divisor r of h let K_r be the unique subgroup
of H of order r, and define

    N_r=sum(e_q for primes q|x whose residue is not in K_r).

If N_r>=h/(2r) for EVERY such r, then -1 belongs to R, so branch A
produces positive integral y,z with 4xyz=p(xy+xz+yz), by `proof.md`.
Equivalently, branch-A failure under -1 in H implies that at least one
odd r|h satisfies N_r<=h/(2r)-1. This is a necessary obstruction,
not a sufficient characterization of failure. Branch B must still be tested.

## Precisely named input (not proved here)

Finite Kneser inequality: for nonempty finite subsets A,B of an abelian
group, with K the stabilizer of their product AB,

    |AB| >= |AK|+|BK|-|K|.

Local source audit before this round checked only a secondary theorem
statement. Primary reference identified, not retrieved or proof-audited:
Martin Kneser, *Summenmengen in lokalkompakten abelschen Gruppen*,
Math. Z. 66 (1956), 88-110, DOI 10.1007/BF01186598.
No network access or new source claim is made in this round.

Its iterated consequence for S=A_1...A_L and K=Stab(S) is

    |S| >= sum_i |A_i K|-(L-1)|K|.

Here is the application audit: pass to H/K. The full projected product
is aperiodic. A period of any nonempty partial product would persist
when multiplied by the remaining sets, hence be a period of the full
product, so all partial products are aperiodic. Apply the two-set
inequality inductively there. The full product S is a union of K-cosets;
thus multiplication by |K| gives the displayed inequality. Each projected
A_i has exactly |A_i K|/|K| elements. No assertion about the stabilizer
of R is substituted for the stabilizer of D.

## Conditional proof

Suppose -1 is absent from R. Then D and -D are disjoint: an intersection
would exhibit a ratio -1. Hence |D|<=h/2. Let K=Stab_H(D).
If |K| were even, the unique involution -1 of H would lie in K,
forcing D=-D, a contradiction. Thus |K|=r is odd, and K=K_r.

D is the product of e_q copies of {1,q} for each prime factor q of x.
This uses multiplicity: each exponent 0,...,e_q is attained by the e_q
binary choices. In the iterated inequality, |{1,q}K| is r for q in K
and 2r for q outside K. Writing L=sum e_q, it yields

    |D| >= r*(L+N_r)-(L-1)*r = r*(1+N_r).

The hypothesized N_r>=h/(2r) would make |D|>h/2. Contradiction.
Moreover the ACTUAL stabilizer K of any branch-A failure gives

    r*(1+N_r)<=h/2, so N_r<=h/(2r)-1.

If -1 is absent from H instead, H has odd order and every prime factor
q of x is a square modulo ell; this is the single-prime quadratic
obstruction. The proof does not transfer unchanged to composite a:
there need not be a unique involution or one odd subgroup of each order.

## Bounded evidence and limitations

In the prescribed 2991 pairs, 941 have prime a and -1 in H. All odd
r|h, the exact K_r, N_r, thresholds, and the actual stabilizer of D
are stored. Among these pairs, 342 fail A; every one has a deficient
K_r, including its actual stabilizer. Of those 342, **89 still succeed
in B** and 253 fail both. This is a direct warning against suppressing B.
Every one of these 941 pairs also passes the finite inequality
|D|>=r*(1+N_r) for its actual stabilizer; this is NOT a proof of Kneser's theorem.

The conditional statement is a one-shift result. The records do not
force saturation at any shift for every p. No cross-shift bridge survives
the two bounded counterchecks in `findings.md`.
